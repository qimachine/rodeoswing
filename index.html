A Typical Tuesday

Here's What the Night Looks Like

It's not fancy. It's country music, a dance floor, and a room full of people who genuinely want to have a good time — and who genuinely want to help each other get better at dancing. Whether you're out for the first time or have been coming for years.

🎓

7–8 PM: The Class

Structured instruction every week in country swing — the footwork, the leads, the follows, and the moves that make social dancing actually fun. We cover new material every week, rotating through beginner concepts and more advanced patterns.

🎵

8 PM–Late: Social Dancing

The music keeps going and the floor opens up. Country swing is the foundation, but the social naturally drifts into other country dance styles as the night gets going. Dance with whoever asks — our regulars make it a point to get everyone out on the floor.

🤠

10+ Years of Experience

Your instructors have been doing this for over a decade. You're in good hands — and they know how to actually explain things, not just demonstrate them.

🌻

People Who Actually Care

The regulars here are invested in each other's dancing. Experienced dancers seek out newer people — not out of obligation, but because helping someone click with a move is genuinely satisfying. It's a community that gets better together.

💃🕺

Do I Need a Partner?

Nope. If you come with a partner, you're welcome to stay together throughout the lesson. If you come on your own, we'll pair you with someone in the opposite role (leader or follower).


During class we rotate partners every few minutes so everyone gets experience dancing with different people. It's the fastest way to improve, meet people, and become comfortable social dancing.

🤔

Is There Line Dancing?

We don't teach line dancing, but it's common to see it break out on the floor when the right song comes on. It's a shared floor, so if you're leading a partner dance, it's on you to steer clear of the line dancers.

New to This?

You Don't Need to Know Anything to Show Up

Seriously. "Beginner" isn't a qualifier for who can come — it's a description of most people when they first walk in. We plan for it every single week.

  • Yes, you can just come and watch — it's free. Pay $10 when you're ready to join in.
  • Class starts from the basics every week — no experience assumed
  • You don't need a partner; we rotate during class
  • The experienced dancers here want to dance with you — it genuinely makes them better too
  • No cowboy boots required — comfortable closed-toe shoes are fine
  • Come alone, come with a friend, come with whoever
  • You will make mistakes. So does everyone. It's part of how you learn.

Good Floor Manners

Dance Floor Etiquette

Partner dancing runs on a few simple courtesies. It's how we keep the floor fun, safe, and comfortable for everyone.

Quick definitions: in partner dancing, the lead initiates and guides the pattern (traditionally the man's role); the follow responds to the lead's signals (traditionally the woman's role).

Asking to Dance

Traditionally leads ask and follows say yes — but anyone can ask anyone. A friendly ask is always welcome.

"No" Is Always Okay

Follows aren't obligated to accept — and leads shouldn't take it personally. "Maybe next song" is a totally fine answer.

You Can Stop Anytime

Either partner can end a dance early, no explanation required. "Thanks, I need some water" is a perfectly good exit line.

Lead with Care

Leads are responsible for the safety of their follow and those around them.

Floor Awareness

Keep half an eye on the couples around you and give line dancers room. A crowded floor just means smaller steps.

Come Floor-Ready

Deodorant and fresh breath go a long way — you'll be dancing close to a lot of different people over the night.

Speak Up

If anything makes you uncomfortable at any point, tell anyone on the instruction team. We want to know.

End With a Thank You

However the dance went, a "thank you" at the end is the one etiquette rule that's truly universal.
